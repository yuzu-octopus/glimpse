### A2. Actual documented/measured limits of the playlist feed

- **Hard cap = 15 entries.** Verified: playlists with ≥15 items return exactly 15 (`PL0vfts4VzfNiIf8KLhDVNJQ-2vVWpTG-L` page≥16 → 15; `PL0vfts4VzfNieTtC_yYSK7M1S5Hz_ffPz` page≥100 → 15; `PL0vfts4VzfNiLaL4tjUeM5awbISg2dovL` page≥18 → 15; `PL0vfts4VzfNiyWLrzZRdF2SUYHiIvxF1G` page≥15 → 15). Playlists below 15 return their full count: measured feed==page for 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8-item playlists. — fetched 2026-09-27 — **primary (empirical)**
- **The feed is the FIRST 15 ITEMS IN PLAYLIST ORDER — not the 15 most recent, and not date-sorted.** This is the key semantic and it is counterintuitive. Three independent proofs:
  1. **Proof via `youtubei.js` full listing (strongest).** `PL0vfts4VzfNhyuLwMtD1_f1hSWo6V_kIY` ("OpenAi"): `youtubei.js` returned **28 items**; the RSS feed returned 15; the script compared them → `rss == first 15 of items? true`. Exact match, same order. — fetched 2026-09-27 — **primary (empirical, reproducible)**
  2. **Proof via page scrape.** `PLbpi6ZahtOH6Blw3RGYpWkSByi_T7Rygb`: playlist page first six videoIds = `Vh4O04Bpovw,5TIp7oVKHq8,7u43Zlj9KK8,InDJvcWtTCw,GM_OV3wP3Yo,NMfFvPkju8c`. RSS feed first six = **identical sequence**. — **primary (empirical)**
  3. **Proof by the "Shorts" playlist (a chronological playlist).** `PL0vfts4VzfNigohKr5sPrkcPFpuZmTe2C` has 48 videos but the feed returns **14 entries, all published 2021-03-20 … 2021-06-29, ascending**. The oldest videos in the playlist, not the newest. The 14th/15th boundary is exactly where the feed stops matching the page. — **primary (empirical)**
- **⇒ Practical rule: for a playlist whose owner ordered it oldest-first (common for "all episodes", conference talks, curricula), the RSS feed returns the OLDEST videos and will look permanently "stale".** A widget built on it will be wrong, not merely incomplete. This is the failure mode to design against.
- **The "Top Trending Videos of the Week" playlist feed looks frozen at 2020 — but it is NOT stale.** Its first 15 videos genuinely are 2020 videos (proved by the page-order match above). The playlist was abandoned; the feed faithfully reflects it. Do not misdiagnose this as feed rot.
- **The 14-vs-15 anomaly is a dropped item, not a different cap.** For the "Shorts" playlist the feed's 14 ids match page positions 1–14 exactly; page position 15 (`XQMVOoPZLYs`, oEmbed confirms it exists: title "Is your memory leaking?", author Fireship) is **absent from the feed**. So: cap 15, minus items YouTube chooses to omit. Root cause of the omission is unverified. — **primary (empirical) + speculative on cause**
- **Reordering IS reflected, but only within the first 15 slots.** Because the feed is a positional window, moving an item into/out of positions 1–15 changes the feed; reordering below position 15 is invisible. Confirmed by the identical feed/page order in the two proofs above. — **primary (empirical)**
- **"Only new additions" is a MIScharacterisation.** The feed is not an incremental "since last fetch" delta — it is a positional snapshot of playlist slots 1–15. Fetching repeatedly yields the same 15 until slots 1–15 change. — **primary (empirical)**
- **CDN caching: `cache-control: public, max-age=900`, `server: YouTube RSS Feeds server`, `content-type: text/xml; charset=UTF-8`, `accept-ranges: none`, no `etag`.** So responses are cached for 15 minutes at the edge; a 404 may also be edge-cached. — https://www.youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=UCsBjURrPoezykLs9EqgamOA — fetched 2026-09-27, `date: Sun, 27 Sep 2026 09:51:21 GMT` — **primary**
- **Feed XML shape** (verified verbatim, channel feed): Atom with `xmlns:yt="http://www.youtube.com/xml/schemas/2015"`, `xmlns:media="http://search.yahoo.com/mrss/"`, `<id>yt:channel:…`, `<yt:channelId>`, per-entry `<yt:videoId>`, `<published>`, `<updated>`, `<media:group>`. A tiny hand-rolled parser is entirely adequate; see B6. — **primary**
- **PubSubHubbub hub is reachable and accepts subscriptions for BOTH channel and playlist feed URLs** — `POST https://pubsubhubbub.appspot.com/subscribe` with `hub.topic=…?channel_id=UCsBjUR…` → **HTTP 202**; with `hub.topic=…?playlist_id=PL0vfts4VzfNhyuLwMtD1_f1hSWo6V_kIY` → **HTTP 202**. `GET https://pubsubhubbub.appspot.com/` → HTTP 200. — fetched 2026-09-27 — **primary for reachability; delivery UNVERIFIED** (202 = "accepted, verification pending"; my callback was `http://example.com/cb` so verification could not complete). **Do not treat 202 as proof of delivery.**
- **No official documented "how many entries" limit exists** for either feed. The 15 figure is measured, not documented. The doc page does not state a limit. — **primary (absence of documentation) + measured**

### B6. Constraint verdict — Bun + TS strict + "no new heavy dependency"

The measurement that decides this: **one `POST` to `youtubei/v1/browse` returned 2,331,501 bytes for one playlist page**, and the package weighs **63 MB / 3,537 files / 15.08 MB unpacked**, with `meriyah` (a full ES2020 JavaScript parser) and `@bufbuild/protobuf` as mandatory transitive deps. A dashboard widget that needs ~28 ids and 4 fields is paying for an entire YouTube client to do it.

**Recommendation, per package:**

- **`youtubei.js` — NOT worth it. "Maintained" but still a heavy dependency, and it buys us nothing we cannot do in ~30 lines.** Evidence: my zero-dependency recursive walk over the same `VL<id>` response produced the **identical 28 ids in identical order** to `youtubei.js`, on Bun, with no package installed. The only things it adds are PO-token workarounds, cipher/decipher for streams, and ~3,500 files. It also *removed* the RSS helpers (`getFeed`, `getVideos`) in v18, so it cannot even replace our feed path. Additional risk: no Bun in CI, no `engines`. **Only worth revisiting if we ever need deciphering/stream URLs or formats the plain walk doesn't expose — i.e. a different product.**
- **`fast-xml-parser` — maintained and the safest of the XML options, but NOT NEEDED.** The feed is a fixed, small Atom shape; we already have an XML helper. 90M weekly downloads means it is a high-value CVE target, and `fast-xml-parser` has a documented history of XXE/entity-expansion issues. We already parse this feed successfully. **Skip.** (Flagged as the pick *if* we ever face genuinely malformed/variable XML.)
- **`rss-parser` — do not add.** Last release 2023-04-11 (3.4 years stale) while the repo churns; it wraps `xml2js` + `entities`, adding two transitive deps to parse a shape we already parse. 980k weekly downloads is mostly transitive usage, not endorsement. **Clear reject.**
- **`youtube-dl-exec` — well maintained, wrong tool.** Requires a `yt-dlp` binary on the host, spawns a subprocess per call, and downloads media metadata far beyond what a widget needs. Reject on the "no heavy dependency / serverless-friendly" rule.
- **`yt-dlp-wrap` — abandoned.** npm last publish 2023-09-13 and the GitHub repo `foxesdocode/yt-dlp-wrap` returns **404 Not Found** (verified 2026-09-27). Dead upstream. Reject.
- **`youtube-search-api` — not a fit.** Search-only by its own description; we have specific ids, not queries. One dep (`axios`) on Node HTTP, unnecessary under Bun. Reject.
- **`ytpl` — abandoned** (repo archived 2024-01-09, npm last publish 2022-03-04). Reject.
- **`node-youtube` — abandoned** (2018, `request`/`request-promise`). Reject.
- **`youtube-extractor` — abandoned** (2021). Reject.
- **`@distube/youtubei.js` — gone from npm.** If any ported code or doc references it, it must be repointed at `youtubei.js`.
- **`youtube.js` — does not exist.** Do not let a migration plan reference it.

**Net dependency recommendation: add nothing.** The entire Part-A answer is reachable with what we have plus one `fetch`. The one thing worth *fixing* is the `@handle` → UC id extraction (A0), which is a regex bug, not a missing package.

## Conflicting evidence

- **"Some small channels (e.g. @Fireship) return HTTP 404 with 0 entries" (task brief) vs my measurement: Fireship `channel_id` and `UU` feeds both return HTTP 200 / 15 entries.** Resolution: the brief's 404 is almost certainly the **UC-id extraction regex picking one of the 17 wrong ids on the page** (A0), not a YouTube-side failure. My first sweep reproduced exactly that failure mode (I got 404 for all 7 channels, including Fireship, purely from a bad extractor), and the re-run with `"externalId"` gave 200/15 for all 7. This conflict is **resolved in favour of "client-side id-extraction bug"** and I consider it the highest-value finding in this angle. Residual: I cannot rule out an *additional* datacenter-IP-specific failure mode, since all my probes ran from a residential IP.
- **"RSS feed only reflects new additions / is an incremental delta" (common assumption) vs measured: it is a positional window of the first 15 playlist slots.** Resolved by the 28-vs-15 `youtubei.js` comparison and the page-order match. Not an assumption — a byte-level order comparison.
- **"Playlist feed is stale for the 2020 playlist" vs measured: the playlist itself is 2020 content at those positions.** Resolved by the page-order match. Do not treat as feed rot.
- **Glance's own comment ("UULF returns empty for many channels") vs measured: UULF returns 404 for 7/7, not "empty for many".** Glance's characterisation is if anything *understating* the failure. The mitigation ("use UULF when include-shorts is false") is not viable as written.

## Gaps

- **All probes were made from a residential IP, not a datacenter IP.** The project runs on a datacenter IP, and datacenter ranges are more likely to be challenged. I could not test that condition. - **No successful PubSubHubbub delivery was ever observed** — only HTTP 202 accept for both channel and playlist topics. Whether YouTube actually pushes is **unverified**; I could not stand up a public callback.
- **Cause of the 14-vs-15 dropped item is unverified.** `XQMVOoPZLYs` exists (oEmbed 200) but is omitted from its playlist's feed. Shorts-related? private? region-locked? Unknown.
- **Only one real "playlist ordered oldest-first" playlist was found** (Fireship "Shorts"). The claim "oldest-first playlists return their oldest videos" rests on that one playlist plus the two order-match proofs. More samples would strengthen it.
- **No primary documentation exists for the `playlist_id` feed at all.** All limits here (15, positional-window semantics, 404 behaviour) are measured, not documented, so they carry no vendor guarantee. A Help-community thread on the 404s is 410 Gone; I could not recover its content.
- **No equivalent 29-playlist sweep was run for a second channel**, so "all normal `PL…` playlists return 200" is evidenced only for Fireship's 29.
- **`youtubei.js` Bun support is observed-once, not contractually guaranteed** (no Bun in CI, no `engines`). My one test passed, including TS import and a live playlist fetch.
- **Datacenter-IP behaviour of `VL<id>` InnerTube with no `key` param is untested** — the single most important thing to verify before relying on it.
